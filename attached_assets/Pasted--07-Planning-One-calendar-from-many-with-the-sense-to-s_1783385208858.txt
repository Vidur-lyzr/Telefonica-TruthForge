# 07 · Planning
### One calendar from many, with the sense to see conflicts coming

> Inherits FOUNDATION + File 01. Unifies the Comms + Brand calendars that today live in Asana, Excel, and elsewhere into a single governed view — with conflict detection and genuine prediction. The RFP's explicit challenge is integrating heterogeneous calendar sources.

---

## 1. The job

Give Comms and Brand a **single view** of everything planned across markets, spot conflicts and gaps before they bite, predict timing risks, and produce the RFP's **10-day forecast** on demand or on schedule. Each event traces back to its source system.

## 3. The event anatomy

Each event/milestone: date · area · type (campaign/milestone/event/publication) · owner · status · **source of origin** (Asana / Excel / …, traceable) · axis pill · market/brand. Click → detail + the source link. Traceability applies to plans, not just documents.

---

## 4. Behaviors

**Unification.** Merge Asana, planning Excels, and other sources into one view — the hard integration the RFP calls out. Each event remembers where it came from.

**Conflict & gap detection.** Two launches, same day, same market → flagged. Activity gaps → surfaced. This is immediate, visible value.

**Predictive capacity (the differentiator).** From history + external signals, the system:
- anticipates **workload by period/area**,
- suggests **optimal dates** avoiding conflicts,
- predicts **future conflicts**,
- cross-references **external signals** (market events, competitor calendar, sector dates) to warn of timing risk/opportunity,
- predicts **delays** and simulates the **cascading effect** of moving one action on the rest of the plan ("move this → these three shift").

**10-day forecast.** On demand or scheduled → generated via the Generate engine (Case 3) with a specific template → Canvas → export → versioned. If scheduled, it goes through the owner's review folder first.

**Embedded chat.** Scoped conversational window: *"what's live next week in Germany?"*, *"is there a conflict if I launch Tuesday?"* → cited answer + suggested actions.

**Edit + sync.** Create/move events per permissions; reflect in the unified view and **sync back to the source (Asana/Excel)** — bidirectional in the ideal case (confirm with Telefónica; read-only fallback if not).

**Alerts.** Upcoming milestones, detected conflicts, plan deviations → owner + Home radar.

---

## 5. States

- **No calendars connected** → "Connect Asana or upload a planning Excel to see everything in one place." The value prop *is* the empty state.
- **Prediction low-confidence** → offered as a suggestion, never asserted ("worth checking" not "will happen").
- **Sync unconfirmed** → default read-only with a clear "synced from Asana · read-only" badge until bidirectional is agreed.
- **Restricted** → you see your area's events; others render as busy/blocked without detail.

---

## 6. Real anchored example

The team organizes the calendar around **Mobile World Congress (MWC) Barcelona** — the densest period of the year: keynote, scheduled releases, launches, brand events, interviews, with all the competition present. Filters: MWC weeks · Spain + global · Comms + Brand. The system detects **two launches on the same day/market** → flags them; cross-references external signals (*"a competitor announces on Tuesday — consider moving your release to Wednesday"*); the user moves the keynote one day → the **cascade simulation** shows which releases, social, and events shift and proposes a readjustment. Embedded chat: *"what do we have during MWC week in Germany?"* The 10-day forecast generates via the Generate engine → Canvas → export, versioned.

---

## 7. Why this page matters

It turns scattered, per-area calendars into one governed, predictive view — and the cascading-effect simulation is the kind of "recommend, don't just describe" capability the RFI promised. Combined with the 10-day forecast flowing through Generate, Planning shows the platform doesn't just *hold* knowledge — it helps the team *act* on time.
